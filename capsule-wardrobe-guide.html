The Capsule Wardrobe Guide

Not a rule about numbers. A way of making sure everything you own works with everything else you own.

The capsule idea gets a bad name because it is usually presented as a purge followed by a shopping list. Neither part is necessary, and the shopping list is usually somebody else's wardrobe.

Start with what you own

Lay out everything you have worn in the last two months. That is already most of your capsule, because people wear a small fraction of what they own and the fraction is remarkably stable. The job is to work out what is holding it together and what is missing, not to start again.

Then look at what has not been worn. For each piece, ask what it goes with. Almost always the answer is “one thing, and that thing is in the wash”.

The compatibility test

A piece earns its place by how many outfits it unlocks, not by how much you like it on the hanger. A plain top that works with five bottoms is worth more than a beautiful one that works with a single skirt, and the arithmetic is not close.

How many of each

Roughly twice as many tops as bottoms, three or four layers, and three pairs of shoes that cover everything you do. Dresses sit outside the arithmetic because each one is a whole outfit; one or two is plenty unless they are most of what you wear.

These numbers are a starting point. A cold climate pushes layers up and tops down; a warm one does the reverse.

Build around a base

Pick the colour most of your shoes and outerwear already are and make that the base for bottoms too. Those are the expensive pieces, and once they agree with each other, tops can be chosen for how much you like them rather than for what they match.

What to do with the orphans

The pieces that only work with one thing are not failures and do not have to go. Move them out of the daily part of the wardrobe. If one keeps pulling you back, the honest answer is usually that it needs a second piece to pair with, which is a far better reason to buy something than a sale.

Keeping it from becoming a uniform

Leave two or three slots deliberately empty, and keep a handful of small things that change an outfit without changing the pieces. Because the base stays constant, those read as decisions rather than as noise, which is the whole point of having a system underneath.
