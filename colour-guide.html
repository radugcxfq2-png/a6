The Colour Guide

Why some combinations look considered and others look like whatever was clean.

Most colour advice is either a seasonal personality quiz or a wheel with no instructions. What actually decides whether an outfit reads as deliberate is simpler and almost entirely mechanical.

Warmth before shade

Every colour leans warm or cool, and mismatched warmth is what makes a pairing feel wrong when both colours are individually fine. A warm cream beside a cool grey looks slightly dirty; the same cream beside a warm stone looks intentional.

Hold two pieces together in daylight and the difference is obvious within a second, even if you cannot name it. That one second of checking replaces most of the theory.

Choose a base and stay with it

The base is whatever covers the largest areas: bottoms, outerwear, shoes. Navy, charcoal, black, camel and olive all work. The choice matters far less than consistency, because a consistent base is what frees the tops.

The three-colour ceiling

A base covering most of the outfit, a second colour doing the work, and one small thing that is neither. Past three, nothing is leading and everything competes, which is what makes an outfit read as accidental even when every piece is good.

Texture is contrast

Two pieces in the same colour but different weaves read as a considered choice. The same colour in the same weave reads as a suit that has lost half of itself. This is why a knitted and a woven piece in identical navy work, and two woven ones usually do not.

What shop lighting hides

Retail lighting is warm, bright and very flattering, and it is why colours look different at home. Take anything you are unsure about to a window or a doorway before you decide. If a shop has no daylight anywhere, that is worth knowing about the shop.

A note on black

Black is the easiest base and the least forgiving next to itself, because different black dyes and fabrics are visibly different colours. Two blacks that do not match look like a mistake, where black with charcoal looks like a choice. If most of your wardrobe is black, the useful move is often to let the second piece be charcoal on purpose.
